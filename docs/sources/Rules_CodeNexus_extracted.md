## Page 5

Three rounds, each asking for something the last did not. The problem statement is released by the organisers one hour before the event begins, so every team starts from the same brief at the same moment. Round 1 and Round 2 results are sent to every team by email, whether or not the team has been selected — you hear from us either way — and the teams that go through receive the instructions for the next stage in the same email. Every participant submits. The project is filed once per member rather than once per team: each person makes their own entry for the round, names the role they held on it — Backend, Frontend, AI / ML, Design / UI-UX, Product / Research or Presentation / Demo — and writes in their own words the part they built. Those two answers are what your certificate prints, so a member who files nothing has nothing recorded and is not issued one. No promotional posts, no social links and no video demonstrations are required; only the documents and project links listed here.

Measured against exactly what you named at Round 2 — not against what turned out to be easier once you started.

**Reliability under normal use** 20 POINTS
Does it hold up when a judge uses it without the team steering? Bad input, an empty state and a repeated attempt all count.

**Usability and real-world fit** 20 POINTS
Could the intended user work it out without a walkthrough? Is it practical in the setting it is meant for?

**Documentation and roadmap** 15 POINTS
Could someone else run this from the repository? And is the next step a credible plan rather than a wish list?

Rounds 1 and 2 are judged in-house by the We Code Coders organising team, so those results come back quickly — and every team that submitted is emailed its Round 1 and Round 2 result, selected or not, without a score or a position.
